**Access.** Every source was accessed on 2026-10-25. "Opened" means the page or text was read, in full or in the part cited. "Snippet" means only a search-engine summary or an abstract was seen; treat such facts as leads. "Unreachable" means the page was blocked or gone.

**Keys and aliases.** The six notes were written separately, and some use different keys for the same work. Each work is listed once, under one key; the other keys are given as aliases, so a key found in any notes file can be looked up here. For example, Posselt's book is POSSELT-1888 in the history notes, POSSELT1888 in the mechanism notes and Posselt1887 in the photos notes: the book is © 1887 and was printed in 1888.

**Page numbers.**
- Posselt is cited by printed page. The Gutenberg figure files are named `i_0NN.jpg`, where NN is the page.
- Barlow is cited by the printed running heads in the OCR. His full-page plates are cited by figure number, because the Internet Archive's page numbers differ from the printed ones by two in that range.
- Kohl's page numbers come from OCR page markers and should be checked against the scan.

**Licences.** "Licence not stated" means all rights reserved by default: facts may be used with a citation, but nothing may be copied. NC licences are treated as incompatible with this project's CC BY-SA 4.0 and Apache-2.0 outputs.

## Public-domain technical books and primary texts

**POSSELT-1888** (aliases POSSELT1888, Posselt1887). E. A. Posselt, *The Jacquard Machine Analyzed and Explained: with an Appendix on the Preparation of Jacquard Cards, and Practical Hints to Learners of Jacquard Designing*. Philadelphia: Pennsylvania Museum and School of Industrial Art, 1888 (© 1887). Text: Project Gutenberg #54193, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54193 (mirror https://archive.org/details/thejacquardmachi54193gut). Scan: Library of Congress / Internet Archive, https://archive.org/details/jacquardmachinea00poss (166 leaves). Opened; full text read. Public domain (US publication before 1931; the LoC scan has "no known copyright restrictions"). Gives: the fully dimensioned 200-hook single-lift head (208 = 26 × 8), Figs. I–XI at ¼ full size, pp. 11–17; common sizes (p. 9); the 400 bottom board (Fig. XXIX); cards and the lacing-frame card sizes (p. 97); card cutting and repeaters (pp. 86–98); comber board, heddles and ties (pp. 19–29); single and double lift (pp. 67–71). Its history section copies Barlow and Willis. Eight plates are kept in `../images/pd-scans/`. *Cited in: history, mechanism, photos.*

**BARLOW-1878** (aliases BARLOW1878, Barlow1878). Alfred Barlow, *The History and Principles of Weaving by Hand and by Power*. London: Sampson Low, Marston, Searle & Rivington; Philadelphia: H. Carey Baird, 1878. https://archive.org/details/historyprinciple00barl (Getty Research Institute scan, 492 leaves). Opened; full OCR searched; pp. 150 and 157 checked on the page images (leaves n165, n172). Public domain (pre-1900). Gives: the 1725/1728/1745 chronology (pp. 33–34); the 1801 model at the CNAM (p. 127); the Willis quotation and the Marin models (pp. 140–142); the 1823 and 1831–32 parliamentary evidence, including Jacquard's own account through Bowring (pp. 142–146); the common hand loom and the weaver's motions (pp. 78–84); the 400-needle hand-loom head (pp. 147–158); harness and point tie (pp. 159–165). Six plates are kept. *Cited in: history, mechanism, photos.*

**KOHL-1873** (alias Kohl1873). Friedrich Kohl, *Geschichte der Jacquard-Maschine und der sich ihr anschliessenden Abänderungen und Verbesserungen nebst der Biographie Jacquards* (History of the Jacquard machine and the modifications and improvements that followed it, with a biography of Jacquard). Prize essay of the Verein zur Beförderung des Gewerbfleisses in Preussen, Berlin, 1873. https://archive.org/details/geschichtederja00kohlgoog (history notes); https://archive.org/details/11155862bsb (photos notes). Opened; German OCR searched; plates not reviewed. Public domain. Gives: the dating argument (the 1801 patent covers the lacs-drawing machine; the card machine most probably 1805, with no patent); the biography (2 February 1804 medal, return to Lyon in 1804, Vaucanson's remains); the "original form" of the head after the *Dictionnaire technologique* vol. XI: 8 knives, four-sided prism, lantern, catches, reserve rows (pp. 31–34). *Cited in: history, photos.*

**BRADBURY-1912** (aliases BRADBURY1912, Bradbury1912). Fred Bradbury, *Jacquard Mechanism and Harness Mounting*, 1912. The mechanism notes give the publisher as Halifax: F. King & Sons, the photos notes as Manchester: John Heywood; not resolved. https://archive.org/details/jacquardmechanis00brad (Smithsonian Libraries scan). Opened; pp. 82 and 245 checked on the page images (leaves n87, n250). Public domain in the US (published abroad before 1931); UK status unknown, because the author's death date was not found, so no figures were kept. Facts only, with citation. Gives: the single-lift machine, ch. VII pp. 67–83, including the measured timing table (p. 82, Fig. 74) and the swan neck (pp. 78–79); double lift (pp. 83–84); load example (p. 170); Verdol and card sizes (pp. 245–249); ties (pp. 269–273); harness problems (pp. 301–302). *Cited in: mechanism, photos.*

**NELSON-1909** (aliases NELSON1909, nelson-gutenberg, ASC1909). H. William Nelson, *Jacquard Machines: Instruction Paper*. Chicago: American School of Correspondence, 1909. Project Gutenberg #50532, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/50532 (also https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/50532/pg50532-images.html). Scan: https://archive.org/details/jacquardmachines00amer. Opened. Public domain in the US. Gives: the 400 machine (416-hole needle board in 52 rows of 8, split 26 + 26); hook forms (Figs. 1–8); cylinder travel 2–3 in and ¼ in clearance at the turn; lift ratios; couplings and lingoes; speeds (90–130 single lift, 170–180 double action); faults; the four machine classes. *Cited in: mechanism, photos, build.*

**BELL-1895** (aliases BELL1895, Bell1895). T. F. Bell, *Jacquard Weaving and Designing*. London and New York: Longmans, Green & Co., 1895. Project Gutenberg #53318, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/53318. Scan: https://archive.org/details/gri_33125001153218 (marked NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT). Opened (text; figures not reviewed page by page). Public domain. Gives: French fine-pitch 16-row cards (Fig. 31); design paper (Fig. 68); the heck (Fig. 48); comber-board construction; lingo weights; card-cutting and repeating speeds; the counterpoise; the French paper machine. *Cited in: mechanism, photos.*

**URE-1839** (alias Ure1839). Andrew Ure, *A Dictionary of Arts, Manufactures, and Mines*. London: Longman, Orme, Brown, Green & Longmans, 1839; article "Jacquard loom", pp. 656–660. https://archive.org/details/b2930345x (Wellcome Library scan, Public Domain Mark). Opened. Public domain (the author died in 1857). Gives: front and side elevations of a hand loom with a Jacquard head and a section of the head (Figs. 566–568); needles, cylinder and cards (Figs. 569–573). Two plates are kept. *Cited in: photos.*

**FOX-1894** (alias fox). T. W. Fox, *The Mechanism of Weaving*, 1894. https://archive.org/details/mechanismofweavi00foxt. Downloaded and searched for speeds only (nothing used); cited as the design basis of Fenton's and Elm-Y's heads. Public-domain scan. *Cited in: mechanism (as "Fox 1894", without a key), value-check.*

**WILLIS-1855.** Rev. R. Willis, "On Machinery and Woven Fabrics", in *Reports on the Paris Universal Exhibition 1855*, Part II, p. 150. Not opened; known only through BARLOW-1878. Public domain. Gives: the root English account of Bouchon, Falcon, Vaucanson and Jacquard, and of the nine Marin models. *Cited in: history.*

**LOVELACE-1843.** L. F. Menabrea, "Sketch of the Analytical Engine invented by Charles Babbage, Esq.", translated with notes by A. A. L. [Ada Lovelace], *Scientific Memoirs* vol. 3 (London: R. & J. E. Taylor, 1843), pp. 666–731. Note A begins on p. 691 and the loom passage is on p. 696 (calculated from the offprint's pagination). Transcriptions: https://www.fourmilab.ch/babbage/sketch.html (opened) and Project Gutenberg #75107, https://gutenberg.org/files/75107/75107-h/75107-h.htm (opened; offprint pagination). Public domain. Gives: "weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers and leaves" (Note A); operation and variable cards (Note B); "backing" the cards to repeat groups (Note C). *Cited in: history, education.*

**BABBAGE-1864.** Charles Babbage, *Passages from the Life of a Philosopher*. London: Longman, Green, 1864. Project Gutenberg #57532, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/57532. Opened; full text searched. Public domain. Gives: the Engine explained through the Jacquard loom (pp. 116–117); the woven portrait in his drawing-room, shown to Prince Albert and the Duke of Wellington (pp. 169–170); the portrait's size, 2 ft 8 in × 2 ft 2 in (pp. 306–307, footnote). *Cited in: history.*
